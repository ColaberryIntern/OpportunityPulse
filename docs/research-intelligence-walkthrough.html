Research Intelligence Expansion

arXiv → buildable product plan · Walkthrough · 2026-05-14 · Phases 1–4 shipped

You handed over the Research Channel Expansion Plan and said "put together a plan to implement this", then "proceed with everything." This is everything — Phases 1 through 4, built, tested, and (Phases 1–3) running on prod.

Opportunity Pulse already watched government contracts, jobs, funding, freelance and news. It now also watches research — arXiv papers, Hugging Face papers, AI-lab blogs — as a first-class channel. But the point was never to just list papers. The point is a pipeline that takes a raw paper and walks it all the way to "here is a product Colaberry could build, here is who would buy it, here is the MVP."

Four phases. Phase 1 live Phase 2 live Phase 3 live Phase 4 shipped

Why add a research channel

Every other channel in Opportunity Pulse is demand — someone already wrote a contract, posted a job, raised a round. Research is supply that hasn't found its demand yet. A paper on agent memory published today is the thing three state IT contracts will be asking for in eight months.

The expansion answers three questions the other channels can't:

  • What's coming? — momentum scoring on research topics surfaces what's accelerating before it's a buzzword.
  • Is it real? — cross-channel matching links a paper to actual paid opportunities already in the system. A paper with 5 linked gov contracts is a different bet than one with zero.
  • What would we build? — the Phase 4 build brief turns a buildable paper into a concrete product idea, architecture sketch, MVP scope and proposal angle.

The pipeline, end to end

Each stage runs on its own daily cron and writes onto the opportunity row, so every later stage builds on the earlier ones.

1 · IngestarXiv / HF papers / AI-lab RSS → opportunities rows, type research
2.1 · SummarizeAI exec summary, build rec, buildable flag, market timing
2.2 · MatchKeyword overlap → links to gov / talent / capital / freelance opps
2.3 · AggregateRebuild author + topic tables, momentum scores
3 · Embed + graphVector embeddings → semantic search; matches → edge table
4 · BriefBuildable paper → product idea / architecture / MVP / pitch

Stages 2.1–4 only ever add keys to aiAnalysis on the opportunity row — nothing is destructive, every stage is idempotent and safe to re-run.

Phase 1 — Research as a first-class channel

Before any intelligence can run, research has to be a real channel — not a bolt-on. Phase 1 threaded research through every layer that already knew about gov contracts and jobs.

What landed

  • 3 keyless ingestion adapters — arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Hugging Face Papers. Each follows the existing BaseAdapter fetch() + transform() contract, registered in ingestion.service.js, seeded as data_sources rows.
  • New opportunity type research — added to the model enum, OPPORTUNITY_TYPES constant, and the channels.service channel list (🔬 Research, indigo).
  • Research-aware classification — classifyResearch() routes a paper to BUILD (on-topic + has a repo/traction), PARTNER (on-topic but early), or IGNORE (off-topic) using a high-priority keyword list.
  • Frontend — research line on the trends chart, sidebar channel link, channel dropdown option, and a ResearchMeta strip on the opportunity card showing authors, citations, upvotes, venue, and GitHub/PDF links.

A research paper now flows through ingestion, classification, the dashboard, and the opportunity list exactly like every other opportunity — it just carries paper-specific metadata in sourceData.

Phase 2.1 — AI research summaries

A raw abstract is written for academics, not for a services company deciding where to spend a quarter. researchSummary.service.js runs each research opp through gpt-4o-mini and produces a business-oriented summary stored on aiAnalysis.research_summary:

FieldWhat it answers
executive_summaryWhat is this, in plain business English?
build_recommendation + buildableCould Colaberry actually ship something from this? (boolean gate for Phase 4)
market_timingOne of too_earlyemergingactivesaturated
competitive_insightWho else is already in this space?

The buildable flag is the important one — it's the gate that decides which papers get a Phase 4 build brief. No point briefing a paper we've already judged we can't ship.

Phase 2.1 feedback

Phase 2.2 — Cross-channel matching

This is the "is it real?" stage. crossChannelMatch.service.js links a research paper to opportunities in other channels — a gov contract, a job posting, a funding round, a freelance project — by keyword and tag overlap.

  • extractTerms() pulls significant words from the paper's title, tags and domains (with a domain stopword list so "learning", "model", "neural" don't match everything).
  • Candidate opportunities are scored by shared-term count; the top 8 with an overlap score ≥ 2 are persisted on aiAnalysis.cross_channel_matches.
  • The candidate query uses Postgres tag-overlap + title regex, capped at 400 rows per paper so it stays cheap.

This is deliberately not embeddings or a graph yet — it's fast keyword overlap. Phase 3 adds the semantic layer on top. The card shows a 🔗 N cross-channel links badge, and these matches become the "demand signal" that Phase 4 grounds its build brief in.

cron 7:00 AM UTC, force=true   (re-runs fully each day because matches go stale as new opps land in other channels).

Phase 2.2 feedback

Phase 2.3 — Author + topic aggregation

Two new tables, rebuilt idempotently from the research opps every day:

  • research_authors — per distinct author name: paper count, summed citation traction, the topics and sources they show up in. "Who is publishing in this space."
  • research_topics — per topic: paper count, a momentum_score from comparing the recent 30-day window against the prior 30 days, a growth_rate, and a commercial_score from the high-priority keyword list. "What's hot, and is it getting hotter."

Momentum scoring is the answer to "what's coming?" — a topic accelerating month-over-month is a leading indicator before it shows up as contract demand.

cron 7:15 AM UTC   research_authors research_topics

Phase 2.3 feedback

Phase 2.4 — Per-source ingestion intervals

The expansion plan called for arXiv to be polled every couple of hours but Semantic Scholar only daily — they have very different publish cadences and rate limits. Rather than one fixed cron, each data_sources row now carries an ingest_interval_minutes value.

  • Seeded: arXiv every 120 min, Hugging Face Papers every 360 min, Semantic Scholar every 1440 min.
  • New research.scheduler.js ticks every 30 minutes and runs only the sources whose interval has elapsed since their last run.

tick every 30 min

Phase 2.4 feedback

Phase 2.5 — Tier-2 adapters

Two more sources, with one honest caveat:

  • Research-blog RSS adapter — a configurable multi-feed adapter (OpenAI / DeepMind / BAIR) using rss-parser. Live on prod.
  • Papers With Code adapter — built to the same contract, but disabled on prod: Meta shut down the public API after the acquisition and it now returns HTML for every endpoint. The adapter stays in the codebase so it's a one-line re-enable if the API ever comes back.

Semantic Scholar's keyless tier also rate-limits hard (429 on the first request) — it's seeded but disabled until a free API key is added. Graceful degradation: the channel works fine on arXiv + HF + blogs alone.

Phase 2.5 feedback

Phase 3 — Semantic search + research graph

Phase 2.2 matched papers to opportunities by literal shared words. Phase 3 adds the meaning layer.

Embeddings + semantic search

  • The AI client gained an embed() method (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small, single or batched).
  • semanticSearch.service.js embeds each research opp and stores the vector as JSONB on the opportunity row. Search embeds the query, then ranks by in-process cosine similarity.
  • findSimilar(id) does "more papers like this one" off the same vectors.

Why JSONB and not pgvector

Production Postgres is the postgres:15-alpine image with no pgvector extension. Adding it would mean a full dump/restore across a musl→glibc collation change — a risky migration for a feature that works fine without it at this data scale. Embeddings are stored as JSONB and cosine similarity runs in-process. It's forward-compatible: if we ever swap the image, the vectors are already there to backfill into a real vector column.

The research graph

  • research_relationships — an edge table. buildRelationships() materializes the Phase 2.2 cross-channel matches into queryable edges (research opp → related opp, with channel + score).
  • buildTopicGraph(topic) assembles a topic into a tree: the topic, its papers, its authors, and its cross-channel connections grouped by channel.

Phase 3 feedback

Phase 4 — Research-to-build briefs

The capstone. Every prior phase exists to feed this one. researchBuildBrief.service.js takes a paper that Phase 2.1 flagged buildable and turns it into a concrete build plan a product strategist could act on.

The AI is given three things: the paper itself, its Phase 2.1 summary, and — crucially — its Phase 2.2 cross-channel matches as a demand signal. It produces, onto aiAnalysis.build_brief:

FieldExample shape
product_ideaThe specific product/service to build from this research.
target_customersWho buys it — grounded in the linked opportunities ("the 3 linked state IT contracts suggest gov procurement is a near-term buyer").
suggested_architectureRough technical shape — what's reused vs. net-new.
mvp_scopeThe smallest version that proves value and ships in ~1 quarter.
proposal_angleThe one-line pitch for a proposal or sales conversation.
confidence0–1, how real + near-term this build is.

The prompt is explicit: be concrete to this paper, no generic "build an AI platform" filler, and if there's no demand signal, say so honestly ("no direct demand signal yet — speculative") rather than inventing one.

The card shows a 🧭 Build brief badge; hovering surfaces the product idea and pitch. The batch is idempotent — it skips papers that already have a brief unless force=true — and admins can trigger it on demand via POST /research/build-briefs.

Key decisions worth knowing

  • JSONB embeddings over pgvector — avoided a risky prod DB image swap; forward-compatible (see Phase 3).
  • Keyword matching before semantic matching — Phase 2.2 is cheap literal overlap; Phase 3 layers meaning on top. Both run; they answer slightly different questions.
  • Everything is additive + idempotent — each stage only adds keys to aiAnalysis; every batch is safe to re-run, and force=true exists where a full refresh is wanted.
  • Graceful source degradation — Papers With Code (dead API) and Semantic Scholar (keyless 429) are seeded but disabled; the channel runs fine on arXiv + HF + blogs.
  • buildable as the Phase 4 gate — only papers the AI judged shippable get a build brief, so we don't spend tokens briefing dead ends.
